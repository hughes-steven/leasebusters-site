How a lease takeover works

A lease transfer moves an existing car lease — its payment, its remaining term, its kilometre allowance — from one approved driver to another. The lender signs off, and both sides win.

Getting out of your lease

  1. 1
    Confirm your lease is transferable

    Most Canadian lenders allow transfers. Check your contract or call your finance company — we list the policies of every major automaker.

  2. 2
    Build your listing

    Payment, months remaining, kilometre position, good photos. Under allowance? Say so — it's a selling point. Over? Offer a cash incentive.

  3. 3
    Field interest

    Qualified shoppers contact you through the marketplace. You choose who moves forward.

  4. 4
    Lender approval & paperwork

    Your finance company credit-checks the new driver and prepares the transfer agreement. Typical turnaround is one to three weeks.

  5. 5
    Hand over the keys

    Once the transfer is registered you have no further obligation — no payments, no insurance, no end-of-lease charges.

Taking over a lease

  1. 1
    Shop the whole deal

    Every listing shows the payment, term, allowance, buyout, and any cash incentive — compare true monthly cost, not sticker price.

  2. 2
    Meet the car

    See it in person or arrange an independent inspection. The kilometre pace on the listing tells you how it's been used.

  3. 3
    Apply with the lender

    Same credit application as a new lease, with the automaker's own finance arm. No dealer in the middle.

  4. 4
    Pay the transfer fee

    Usually $395–$650, set by the lender. That's the only mandatory cost — no down payment, no freight, no admin fees.

  5. 5
    Drive

    Collect any seller incentive at signing. At term end, return the car or exercise the buyout — your choice.

The math that makes it work

New leases front-load cost: down payment, freight and PDI, admin fees, first-year depreciation. A takeover skips all of it. You pay the lender's transfer fee, inherit the negotiated payment, and any seller incentive drops your effective cost further.

Sellers price incentives against what exiting the lease would otherwise cost them — early termination on a typical lease runs $3,000–$8,000, so paying you $1,500 to take it over is the cheap way out.

Frequently asked

Who is responsible for wear and tear from before I took over?

The lender's end-of-term inspection doesn't distinguish between drivers — which is why you inspect the car before committing. Document existing damage, and factor it into the incentive you negotiate. Some lenders offer a pre-transfer inspection for exactly this reason.

Can I negotiate the incentive?

Absolutely. The incentive is a private arrangement between you and the seller — the lender isn't involved. Sellers under time pressure are often flexible.

What happens to the original driver's security deposit?

It varies by lender: some refund it to the original lessee at transfer, others carry it through to term end. It's spelled out in the transfer agreement before anyone signs.

Can a business take over a personal lease, or vice versa?

Most lenders allow it with the appropriate credit application. Businesses often use takeovers to cover short-term vehicle needs without committing to a full term.

Which provinces does this work in?

Lease transfers work across Canada. The car's registration moves to your province like any used-vehicle move, and insurance is arranged in your name before the handover.
