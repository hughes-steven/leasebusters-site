Why the ledger favours a takeover

Exit without penalties

Breaking a lease costs $3,000–$8,000 in early-termination charges. Transferring it to an approved driver costs a lender fee of a few hundred dollars — and your obligation ends the day the papers are signed.

Commit to months, not years

Take over a lease with 6 to 30 months left instead of signing for four years. Try the brand, cover a season of life, then hand the car back or exercise the buyout.

Incentives put you ahead on day one

Motivated sellers add cash at transfer — $1,000 to $5,000 on this marketplace — which drops your effective monthly payment below anything a dealer can print.

Good questions

Is a lease takeover actually legal?

Yes. Most Canadian lease contracts explicitly allow transfers, subject to the lender's approval of the new driver. The automaker's finance arm processes the paperwork and the new driver assumes the contract on its original terms.

What does it cost to take over a lease?

Typically just the lender's transfer fee — usually $395–$650 — plus licensing and insurance. No down payment, no freight and PDI, no dealer fees. If the seller offers a cash incentive, you come out ahead on day one.

What if the seller went over their kilometre allowance?

Every listing shows the odometer against the pro-rated allowance, so you know before you commit. Sellers who are over pace usually offset it with a cash incentive — and you can negotiate.

Do I need good credit?

You'll be approved by the same finance company that approved the original lease, with the same criteria they'd use for a new one. If you'd qualify at the dealership, you'll qualify here.
